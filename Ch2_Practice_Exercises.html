Hard level

Exercise 11: Leap year

Read a year. Print "Leap year" or "Not leap year". A year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4 but not by 100, or it is divisible by 400. Use a logical expression and the conditional operator.

Test data: 2024: leap. 1900: not leap. 2000: leap. 2023: not leap.

Exercise 12: Reverse a 3-digit number

Read a 3-digit integer and print it reversed.

Test data: 123 gives 321. 405 gives 504. 120 gives 21 (explain why).

Exercise 13: Distance between two points

Read the coordinates (x1, y1) and (x2, y2). Print the distance √((x2−x1)² + (y2−y1)²) with 2 decimals. Use the math library.

Exercise 14: Wrapping around

Test data: a = 8, b = 3 give 0, 11, 11, -9, 32, 4, and even.

Part 2: Operator Precedence, Worked by hand

No program needed. Use pen and paper, show your steps, and use the table below. Then check yourself on the solutions page.

Precedence table (highest first)
Level Operators Order
1 ++ -- (postfix) left to right
2 ! ++ -- (prefix), sizeof right to left
3 * / % left to right
4 + - left to right
5 < <= > >= left to right
6 == != left to right
7 && left to right
8 || left to right
9 ? : right to left
10 = += -= *= /= %= right to left
11 , left to right
Rules to remember
  • Brackets ( ) are done first.
  • Operators on the same level follow the direction in the last column.
  • Relational and logical results are 1 (true) or 0 (false). In arithmetic they behave as 1 and 0.
  • Assignment operators (including +=, -=, *=, /=, %=) are the lowest. The whole right side is worked out first.
  • && and || stop early: if the left side already decides the answer, the right side is not evaluated, so its ++ or -- never happens.
  • Dividing two integers drops the fraction.
